ABSTRACT
The Fe
40
Co
20
Cr
20
Ni
20 
high-entropy alloy (HEA) exhibits high ductility, good corrosion resistance, and relatively 
low cost, but its low strength limits engineering applications. In this work, based on the Fe
40
Co
20
Cr
20
Ni
20 
high- 
entropy alloy, Si and Al were added simultaneously to adjust the stacking fault energy (SFE) and promote BCC- 
phase precipitation, respectively, thereby achieving the synergistic operation of the TWIP/TRIP effects and 
multiple strengthening mechanisms. The results show that as the Al and Si content increase, the alloy gradually 
evolves from a single-phase FCC structure to an FCC + BCC dual-phase structure. Appropriate additions of Al and 
Si enhance strength and ductility simultaneously, whereas excessive additions increase strength at the expense of 
plasticity loss. Among all compositions, the homogenized alloy with the addition of 7 (at. %) Al and 5 (at. %) Si 
(abbreviated as Al
7
Si
5
-HO alloy) possesses an optimal combination of mechanical properties. Compared to the 
base alloy, the room-temperature tensile properties are significantly enhanced, with increases of 121% in ulti -
mate tensile strength (to 743 MPa) and 33% in elongation at break (to 56%). At 77 K, the tensile strength of 
Al
7
Si
5
-HO alloy further increases to 1127 MPa. Lowering the temperature leads to a reduction in the SFE, and the 
deformation mechanism transitions from a combination of dislocation slip and the TWIP effect at room tem -
perature to a mechanism dominated by TWIP and supplemented by TRIP at cryogenic temperatures. The analysis 
indicates that the overall strengthening mainly comes from grain boundary strengthening and precipitation 
strengthening, accompanied by a small amount of solid solution strengthening. This study provides a useful 
compositional design strategy for the development of low-cost, high-performance HEAs.
1. Introduction
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) have become a focus of research, stem -
ming from their unique compositional flexibility and outstanding me -
chanical performance [ 1 – 3 ]. Face-centered cubic (FCC) HEAs generally 
exhibit favorable plasticity at room and cryogenic temperatures; how -
ever, the comparatively low strength remains a limiting factor, impeding 
their application in scenarios requiring high load-bearing capacity [ 4 , 5 ]. 
Established methodologies for improving alloy strength encompass 
precipitation strengthening, solid-solution strengthening, 
grain-boundary strengthening, and work hardening. Recent studies have 
demonstrated that optimized processing routes can construct heteroge -
neous lamellar structures in non-eutectic high-entropy alloys, thereby 
simultaneously enhancing strength and ductility [ 6 ]. Furthermore, 
directional solidification combined with aging treatment can achieve 
bidirectional precipitation in dual-phase high-entropy alloys, which 
simultaneously tailors the nanoprecipitates in both the FCC and BCC 
phases, significantly reducing the property mismatch between the two 
phases and thus overcoming the strength – ductility trade-off [ 7 ]. How -
ever, compared with complex thermomechanical processing routes, the 
tuning of alloy composition to achieve strength – ductility synergy is 
more convenient.

Since the advent of the HEAs concept, a wide variety of alloying 
additions have been exploited to tailor their performance metrics, 
including hardness, ultimate tensile strength, fatigue tolerance, wear 
resistance, ferromagnetic characteristics, electrical conductivity, and 
electrochemical performance [ 8 – 15 ]. Among them, one of the most 
frequently used alloy elements in transition metal high-entropy alloys is 
the Al element. The Al content is crucial for controlling the phase 
composition, promoting the formation of BCC-based phases and influ -
encing their degree of ordering [ 16 ], thereby enhancing mechanical 
properties. Compared to common transition-metal elements, Al pos -
sesses a larger atomic radius, this also causes pronounced lattice 
distortion, impedes dislocation motion, and increases strength. He et al. 
[ 17 ] demonstrated that Al addition progressively drives the FeCo -
NiCrMn system from an FCC solid solution through an FCC + BCC 
duplex phase to a fully BCC structure, highlighting Al's crucial 
strengthening role. Moreover, Al can promote precipitation [ 18 ], 
thereby contributing additional precipitation strengthening. Kenta et al. 
[ 19 ] added a trace amount of N (0.3 at.%) to the Co
20
Cr
20
Fe
34
Mn
20
Ni
6 
medium-entropy alloy. The nitrogen addition enhanced the 
solid-solution strengthening effect and simultaneously produced pro -
nounced strengthening. Chen et al. [ 20 ] regulated the Nb content in a 
single-phase BCC structure to exploit localized solute pinning effects, 
which effectively suppress dynamic recovery and recrystallization dur -
ing high-temperature deformation, thereby significantly enhancing the 
high-temperature strength while retaining favorable ductility. Si is also 
a commonly used alloying element in high-entropy alloys, which can 
significantly reduce the SFE of the alloys. The deformation behavior of 
FCC HEAs depends critically on intrinsic SFE. Li et al. [ 21 ] reported that 
Si addition in the CoCrNi medium-entropy alloy enhances strength via 
increased lattice distortion. Moreover, the addition of Si reduces SFE and 
facilitates deformation twinning and stress-induced phase trans -
formation from FCC to HCP.
The Fe
40
Co
20
Cr
20
Ni
20 
high-entropy alloy was fabricated in this work 
via increasing the Fe content of the CoCrFeNi alloy, resulting in a single- 
phase FCC structure. Increasing the Fe content not only reduces the 
material cost, but also allows Fe to act as an FCC stabilizer [ 22 ], thereby 
ensuring the ductility of the alloy. In the preliminary work of our 
research group, Al and Si elements were added to the Fe
40
Co
20
Cr
20
Ni
20 
alloy, respectively, and a moderate addition of Al and Si elements 
notably enhanced both the strength and ductility of the alloy. Al en -
hances strength by promoting BCC phase formation, whereas Si im -
proves plasticity by reducing the SFE, thereby activating the TRIP and 
TWIP effects. Recent studies have demonstrated that a favorable com -
bination of strength and ductility can be achieved in high-entropy alloys 
through the addition of Al alone [ 23 ]. In the present work, however, Al 
is introduced primarily to promote the precipitation of secondary pha -
ses; nevertheless, it is also known that Al addition tends to increase the 
SFE [ 24 ], which may suppress the activation of TWIP and TRIP effects. 
To counteract this undesirable effect, Si is simultaneously added to 
lower the SFE, thereby preserving the deformation mechanisms essential 
for enhanced mechanical performance. However, the synergistic effects 
of Al and Si co-addition remain largely unexplored in FCC-structured 
high-entropy alloys. Therefore, this paper uses Fe
40
Co
20
Cr
20
Ni
20 
as the 
base alloy; both Al and Si were co-added to the alloy to enhance its 
strength and ductility synergistically. Accordingly, FeCoCrNiAlSi series 
high-entropy alloys were developed; the strengthening mechanisms 
operative in these alloys under room and cryogenic temperatures have 
been systematically studied.

4.2. Deformation and strengthening mechanisms
Before the quantitative analysis of the strengthening mechanisms, it 
is necessary to first elucidate the intrinsic relationship among the 
various morphologies of the BCC precipitates and their interfacial 
characteristics with the FCC matrix. As described above, the BCC phase 
in the as-cast state exhibits an interdendritic morphology, which arises 
from the segregation of Al and Ni solutes in the interdendritic regions 
during non-equilibrium solidification [ 53 ]. During homogenization 
annealing, the blocky BCC phase decomposes, and some Al and Ni atoms 
diffuse into the grain interiors, leading to the formation of rod-like or 
spherical precipitates. Simultaneously, the remaining fraction in the 
interdendritic regions transforms into long strip-like precipitates 
distributed along the grain boundaries. It is noteworthy that these pre -
cipitates with distinct morphologies have the same crystal structure and 
chemical composition, and their morphological differences arise solely 
from divergent evolutionary pathways under different thermodynamic 
and kinetic conditions.
To investigate the interfacial coherency between the BCC pre -
cipitates of different morphologies and the FCC matrix, TEM analyses 
were conducted on both types of precipitates. Based on HRTEM imaging 
and corresponding FFT diffractograms ( Fig. 11 ), it can be determined 
that regardless of whether the BCC phase is particle or long strip-like, a 
specific crystallographic orientation relationship exists between the FCC 
matrix and the BCC phase, forming a typical semi-coherent interface 
[ 54 ]. The FFT ( Fig. 11 ) analysis reveals that the FCC phase is oriented 
along the [111] zone axis, while the BCC phase is oriented along the 
[110] zone axis, satisfying the classical Kurdjumov – Sachs orientation 
relationship [ 55 ]. The interfacial region exhibits broad dark contrast 
bands ( Fig. 11 ), which originate from the local periodic strain fields and 
interfacial misfit dislocation arrays induced by the substantial lattice 
mismatch between the two phases [ 56 ]. Since both spherical and long 
strip-like BCC precipitates exhibit similar semi-coherent interfacial 
characteristics with the FCC matrix, this provides a microstructural basis 
for the subsequent unified application of the Orowan model in esti -
mating the precipitation strengthening contribution.
The yield strength of polycrystalline materials can be decomposed

dislocation strengthening. Thus, the overall strength is commonly

to strengthening through two primary mechanisms. First, its larger 
atomic radius (compared to Co, Cr, Fe, and Ni) induces lattice distortion 
in the FCC matrix, leading to solid-solution hardening. Second, Al pro -
motes the precipitation of a BCC phase, which enhances strength via 
precipitation hardening. The phase constitution of the Al

In addition, higher yield strength of the BCC precipitates compared 
to the FCC matrix thus enables them to act as a strengthening phase.

dislocation motion, but can also serve as a new dislocation source to 
promote dislocation multiplication and propagation, thereby retaining 
good plasticity while strengthening the alloy. This mechanism provides 
important support for the excellent strength – ductility balance achieved

enhancing both strength and plasticity.

flected in the formation of mechanical twins and the HCP martensite 
from the FCC phase. The exceptional cryogenic ductility of the M   HO

5. Conclusions
This work designed and developed a FeCoCrNiAlSi-series high-en -
tropy alloy (HEA), followed by a systematic investigation into the syn -
ergistic effects of Al and Si on its microstructure and mechanical 
properties. The principal findings are summarized as follows:
The addition of Al and Si facilitates the precipitation of the BCC 
phase, forming a dual-phase FCC + BCC microstructure. Elemental 
partitioning is observed: Al and Ni enrich the BCC phase, while Si resides 
primarily in the FCC matrix. The BCC phase produced in its original cast 
form appears as interdendritic stripes. After homogenizing, the strip-like 
BCC partially decomposes into intragranular spherical or nearly spher -
ical particles, as well as strip-like particles along the grain boundary.
The Al
7
Si
5
-HO alloy exhibits an outstanding strength-ductility com -
bination at both room temperature and 77 K. At 77 K, a UTS of 1127 MPa 
is achieved, attributed to the synergistic action of cryogenically induced 
TWIP and TRIP effects.
Experimental observations corroborate the calculated SFE values, 
underscoring the decisive role of SFE in deformation mechanism control.
Quantitative analysis indicates that the yield strength mainly origi -
nates from grain boundary strengthening and precipitation strength -
ening, while solid solution strengthening contributes relatively little.
During deformation, the BCC phase strengthens the alloy not only 
through the Orowan mechanism, but also by promoting coordinated 
plasticity between the two phases, consequently alleviating the long -
standing strength – ductility trade-off dilemma.
This work establishes compositional regulation as a viable route to 
achieve multi-mechanism synergistic strengthening, thereby offering 
both theoretical and experimental guidance for developing cost- 
effective, high-performance HEAs.
