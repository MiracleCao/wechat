ABSTRACT
The relatively low bonding strength of Al laminates usually leads to interlayer cracks, which can 
cause catastrophic failures of structural components during service life, restricting their further 
application in modern industry. In this work, inspired by the natural hard – soft synergy of fish- 
scale structure, a wavy interlocking interface with a mineral – collagen fiber-like (MCFL) struc -
ture was designed to enhance the bonding strength. Therein, the wavy interlocking interface was 
obtained through non-uniform compression of mineral-like (ML) particles and a soft aluminum 
layer during hot rolling, while the MCFL structure was achieved by introducing ML particles and 
the inhomogeneous precipitation of Zn-Al phases that form collagen fiber-like (CFL) compliant 
domains. Notably, the interfacial fracture modes of the new laminates shifted from flat to 
bifurcating, significantly increasing the bonding strength to 52.6 MPa, which is almost 44.46% 
higher than that of the corresponding traditional laminate. The experimental observations and 
finite element method-crystal plasticity-phase field (FEM-CP-PF) simulations confirm these re -
sults. The enhanced bonding strength is mainly attributed to the transition from a flat/straight 
interfacial fracture mode to a zigzag fracture mode. The zigzag fracture mode consists of two 
characteristic crack-propagation forms: crack branching along the ML/ZnAl ₃ interface and crack 
bypassing around ML particles. In addition, local repeated crack deflection within CFL-related 
grain domains further increases the tortuosity of the crack path. Therefore, the interfacial 
design may serve as an effective method to enhance the bonding strength of Al laminates and 
further popularize laminated structures with other components.

et al., 2023 ; Zhang et al., 2023 ; Neogi and Janisch, 2022 ; Zhou et al., 2023 ). Hence, there is an urgent need to develop Al-laminated 
alloys with high interfacial bonding strength to meet the requirements of modern industry.
To enhance the interfacial bonding strength of laminates, many efforts have been made in processing technology, (e.g., surface 
treatment, rolling process optimization, and diffusion annealing) ( Fu et al., 2022 ; Gao et al., 2025 ; Kim et al., 2021 ; Wang et al., 2024 ; 
Zhang et al., 2024d ). For example, Fu et al. (2022) reported that proper surface-state regulation, specifically steel-side oxidation 
combined with cold rolling, can effectively improve the bonding strength of Al/steel composite plates by promoting oxide cracking, 
fresh metal exposure, local mechanical interlocking, and elemental diffusion at the interface. Gao et al. (2025) showed that optimizing 
the differential-temperature rolling process can produce Ti/Al composite plates with high bonding strength and good flatness, where 
enhanced reduction facilitates interfacial grain refinement and alleviates stress concentration, thereby improving interfacial bonding 
strength. Kim et al. (2021) reported that diffusion annealing reduces residual stress and promotes the formation of strengthening 
phases, thereby enhancing metallurgical bonding and improving the interfacial strength of Al/Cu composite plates. According to the 
above studies, the laminate interface can be optimized through metallurgical bonding. However, without a doubt, the flat interface 
produced by current processing technology makes it prone to tearing fractures ( Fig. 1 (a)) ( Hern ´andez-Escobar et al., 2024 ; Li et al., 
2023c ; Liu et al., 2021 ; Zhang et al., 2024b ). Further improvement in the bonding strength of laminates requires evaluation.
In addition to processing technology, the design of a three-dimensional spatial interface has emerged as another effective method to 
improve the bonding strength of laminates ( Cai et al., 2025 ; Cheng et al., 2024 ; Li et al., 2023 ; Luo et al., 2025 , 2022 ). Unlike the 
traditional flat interface, a newly formed wavy interface can be created by the current three-dimensional spatial interface design ( Paul 
et al., 2022 ; Yang et al., 2023 ; Zhang et al., 2022 ). For instance, Yang et al. (2023) indicated that the wavy interface originated from 
non-uniform impact deformation during explosion welding, which can improve bonding strength by optimizing processing parame -
ters. Paul et al. (2022) noted that optimizing explosion welding and subsequent annealing can tailor the interfacial morphology of Al

detrimental to the environment ( Fig. 1 (b)), which is a significant disadvantage for modern industry ( Chu et al., 2022 ). Hence,

Essentially, insufficient interfacial bonding promotes shear-crack initiation and subsequent propagation, leading to laminate 
failure ( Zhang et al., 2023e ). In natural fish scales, a tightly bonded hard-soft architecture, typically consisting of mineralized hard 
constituents and collagen fiber-rich compliant interlayers, efficiently dissipates shear energy by enforcing crack deflection, branching, 
and interfacial sliding rather than allowing a straight-through fracture path. Inspired by this concept, we ask whether introducing a 
mineral-collagen fiber-like (MCFL) microstructure into the interlayer can reduce the effective shear-driving energy by promoting crack 
bifurcation and increasing crack-path tortuosity, thereby improving the bonding strength of Al laminates.
Inspired by fish-scale hard-soft synergy, we introduce a ceramic-particle-strengthened interface featuring an MCFL microstructure 
into Al laminates. Firstly, the hard constituent is realized by mineral-like (ML) ceramic particles (i.e., SiC particles). Recent studies 
have shown that hard ceramic building blocks can promote pronounced crack deflection and increase crack-path tortuosity, thereby 
enhancing energy dissipation during fracture ( Lu et al., 2025 ). Secondly, the compliant interlayer analog is realized by inhomogeneous 
precipitation of Zn-Al phases, forming collagen fiber-like (CFL). Lee et al. (2025) found that in Al-Zn-Mg-Cu alloys, the evolution of 
precipitate type, size, and number density strongly governs the local mechanical response and fracture resistance. Zhu et al. (2024)
reported that in Al-Zn-Mg-Cu-(Ag) alloys, Ag addition refines the precipitates and narrows the PFZs, which helps preserve fracture 
elongation and improve structural stability; such a heterogeneous precipitate arrangement is analogous to a collagen fiber-like (CFL) 
distribution. Han et al. (2024) pointed out that in Al-Li alloys, stress concentration will be formed at the interface between CFL 
sub-grains and recrystallization zones, which will lead to the generation of crack sources. Therefore, the collagen fiber-like (CFL) 
domains formed by non-uniform precipitation in different alloys significantly affect the material's deformation resistance through 
multiple mechanisms.
Furthermore, during the rolling process (fabrication), the non-uniform distribution of hard ceramic particles may promote non- 
uniform deformation of laminates due to differences in the deformation resistance between Al plates and ceramic particles, thereby 
yielding a wavy, three-dimensional interface. However, previous studies suggest that the introduction of ceramic particles may 
facilitate crack formation and propagation at the laminate interface, thereby reducing interfacial bonding strength ( Ran et al., 2024 ; 
Zhang et al., 2024c ). Whether the new interface improves the laminates' bonding strength remains unknown.
The finite element method (FEM) can be widely used to simulate stress and strain fields, predict crack propagation behavior during 
deformation ( Shen et al., 2025 ; Tancogne-Dejean et al., 2021 ; Zhang et al., 2023c ; Zhao et al., 2022 ), and thereby further illustrate the 
bonding-strengthening mechanisms of the laminates. Shen et al. (2025) integrated FEM simulations with detailed interfacial char -
acterization to analyze the evolution of thermal stress in LPBF-fabricated eutectic ceramic composites, identify 
maximum-principal-stress-driven macro-cracking and interfacial-energy-mismatch-induced micro-cracking, and elucidate the un -
derlying mechanisms of crack formation. Zhao et al. (2022) combined crystal plasticity, XFEM, and cohesive zone modeling to simulate 
fatigue crack propagation across grain boundaries, highlighting the capability of FEM-based methods for describing 
microstructure-sensitive crack growth. Obviously, FEM plays a key role in understanding crack initiation and propagation paths. 
However, according to the FEM simulation, understanding crack propagation remains at a relatively macro level. It is essential to 
examine crack propagation mechanisms thoroughly.
To comprehensively assess microscopic crack propagation mechanisms, a coupled CP-PF model has emerged as the preferred 
choice for metallic materials. Li et al. (2022) described the dislocation behaviors and crack evolution of tungsten by coupling the 
crystal plasticity constitutive relation and phase-field (CP-PF) model, with the coupled model uncovering tungsten ’ s brittle-ductile 
transition (BDT) and four typical temperature-dependent fracture processes. Vakili et al. (2022) adopted a coupled crystal 
plasticity-phase field (CP-PF) model to describe the ductile fracture of the aluminum matrix and the brittle fracture of Mg
2
Si pre -
cipitates in aluminum alloys, predicting the effects of precipitate morphology, orientation, and matrix critical energy release rate on 
damage evolution. Xiong et al. (2025) employed a nonlocal CP-PF model (with dislocation flux and GNDs) to characterize MMCs' crack 
initiation/propagation. Furthermore, they revealed the effects of particle distribution (in-grain/on-boundary) and strength on the 
ductility of MMCs. It is certain that the CP-PF model enables a deeper understanding of damage mechanisms, i.e., their interaction with 
the microstructure under various loading conditions, providing a theoretical basis for tailoring the microstructure and optimizing 
mechanical properties.
In this work, aiming to develop lightweight and high-strength laminated structural materials for energy conservation, a new 
interface, i.e., a wavy interlocking interface with an MCFL structure, was designed to enhance the bonding strength of Al-laminated 
alloy. Initially, the wavy interlocking interface was obtained by the non-uniform compression of hard SiC particles and a soft aluminum 
layer during hot rolling. At the same time, the MCFL structure can be achieved by adding SiC phases (ML) and by inhomogeneous 
precipitation of Zn-Al phases (CFL) during aging treatment. To further analyze the crack propagation law and damage mechanism, this 
study, based on finite element simulation (FEM), also introduced crystal plasticity-cohesive zone model (CP-PF) simulations, estab -
lishing a dual simulation system of “ FEM + CP-PF simulations ” . The combined results of experimental observations and dual simu -
lations indicate that the shear interfacial fracture mode of the new interface is “ bifurcation ” rather than the traditional flat mode. The 
“ bifurcation ” fracture mode, originating from the additive effect of strengthening, including the wavy interlocking interface and MCFL 
structure, causes the significant energy dissipation of crack propagation, thereby achieving an increase of bonding strength to 52.6 
MPa, 44.46% higher than the corresponding traditional one. The results are expected to provide a basic theoretical framework and 
technical support for developing lightweight laminates with high interfacial bonding strength for applications in the aerospace,

2. Materials and methods
2.1. Materials
The aluminum alloy plates were commercial cold-rolled Al-Cu-Mg alloy with the sizes of 150 mm × 100 mm × 6 mm and 130 mm ×
80 mm × 2 mm from Aluminum Corporation of China, with chemical compositions as follows: Cu: 3.5, Mg: 1.4, Mn: 0.4, impurity 
elements: ~0.2, and balanced Al (wt %). Among them, the surface of a 130 mm × 80 mm × 2 mm cold-rolled Al-Cu-Mg alloy is coated 
with a ~50 μ m-thick layer of pure aluminum. The SiC and ZnAl ₃ powders were supplied by Changsha Tianjiu Metal Materials Co., Ltd. 
(Changsha, China), and both powders had a nominal particle size of approximately 100 μ m.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Interface bonding behavior
The bonding behavior of the ML-CFL laminated structure was evaluated using shear testing in combination with multiscale 
microstructural characterization. The results show that the interfacial strength depends on the relative proportions of ZnAl ₃ and SiC in 
the intermediate layer, reaching a maximum of approximately 52.6 MPa in the 3 – 1 MPLC configuration. SEM, EPMA, and TEM an -
alyses reveal the formation of a Zn-Al reaction layer, the precipitation of fine Zn-Al eutectoid phases along grain boundaries, and dense 
atomic-scale bonding at the SiC/ZnAl ₃ interface. These coupled effects-including grain-boundary pinning, diffusion-induced interfacial 
reactions, and the mechanical obstruction provided by the ML-CFL architecture-collectively enhance the interfacial bonding and 
structural reliability of the laminated composite.

3.2. Formation and mechanism of interlamellar structure
Based on the above macro-micro interfacial morphology characterization results, the interlayer structure of the research system 
primarily consists of Zn-Al eutectoid phase and ML particles. The relationship between microstructure and inter-lamellar structure can 
be systematized as follows. Firstly, the Zn-Al eutectoid phase forms a precipitation network with a typical CFL precipitation domain 
during non-equilibrium solidification. Secondly, the introduced SiC reinforcement particles are dispersed in the matrix as an ML hard- 
particle distribution. This dual-phase, synergistic distribution mode further tailors the interfacial microstructure, as shown below. (I) 
The non-uniform precipitation of the Zn-Al eutectoid phase induces the formation of a unique bimodal grain structure; (II) SiC- 
reinforced particles exert a directional extrusion effect on the pure aluminum layer during thermomechanical processing, thereby 
constructing a wavy interface morphology with three-dimensional interlocking characteristics. Therefore, this section will mainly 
explain the formation and mechanisms of the key microstructures, including the MCFL structure, the wavy interlocking interface, and 
the bimodal grain structure.
3.2.1. Formation of MCFL structure
The MCFL structure was constructed based on the synergistic effect of SiC particles and Zn-Al eutectoid phase. Fig. 8 illustrates the 
backscattering electron (BSE) images of Al laminated alloys with different “ ML ” particle contents. When ML reinforcement particles

of the CFL structure transforms from straight to curved, with the distribution area decreasing ( Fig. 8 (b, c)). Moreover, from the BSE 
morphology images of the 3 – 1MPLC sample, it can be observed that there are no cracks present at the interface between ML particles

3.2.2. Formation of a wavy interlocking interface induced by an ML
According to Fig. 6 (c), the introduction of the ML phase results in a wavy, interlocking interface. To further analyze the relationship 
between the interlayer and the interface of the Al-Cu-Mg matrix layer, the BSE images and EPMA element distribution of the PPLC,

39.75 μ m (PPLC), 39.83 μ m (3 – 1MPLC), and 38.53 μ m (1 – 1MPLC), respectively, as shown in Fig. 9 (a-c). These values indicate that the 
thickness of the pure Al layer remains relatively stable after processing. Moreover, the schematic illustration in Fig. 9 (d) further shows 
that the soft pure Al layer provides a deformable region for constructing a wavy interlocking interface between the intermediate layer 
and the Al-Cu-Mg matrix.
The formation mechanism of this special interface morphology can be systematically explained as follows: with the increase of the 
content of ML particles, the extrusion probability of the soft pure Al layer during the rolling process is significantly increased, resulting 
in the interface morphology gradually evolving from the initial flat state to a curved structure with periodic wave-like, as shown in 
Fig. 10 . Specifically, under the action of rolling compressive stress, the ML particles gather at the interface and act as rigid components 
to produce directional plastic deformation on the pure Al layer, and finally form a wavy interlocking interface with a wavelength of 
about 50 ~ 100 μ m and a wave height of 10 ~ 30 μ m. The strengthening mechanism of this wavy interlocking interface is mainly 
reflected in three aspects: (I) enhancing the mechanical interlocking effect by increasing the surface area ( Zhang et al., 2025b ); (II) 
Changing the crack propagation path to consume more energy for crack propagation ( Cheng et al., 2024 ); (III) Optimizing the interface 
stress distribution and reducing the stress concentration ( Zhang et al., 2023b ). The experimental results ( Fig. 5 ) show that the interface 
shear strength increases from 36.39 MPa (flat interface) to 52.6 MPa (wavy interlocking interface), representing a 44.46% increase.

3.3. Crack propagation mechanism
Shear strength is closely related to crack propagation, which directly reflects a material's ability to resist failure under external 
stress ( Ran et al., 2024 ). Various crack propagation modes not only indicate the direction and behavior of the crack, but also reflect the 
energy consumption due to shear stress, correlating with its interfacial bonding strength ( Liu et al., 2025 ; Zhao et al., 2023b ). 
Therefore, we will analyze the interfacial bonding strength of laminated aluminum alloys using shear-crack behavior.
3.3.1. Macroscopic 'zigzag' extension mechanism
A key question is whether ML particle structures influence fracture behavior during shear fracture in laminated composites. In this 
regard, Fig. 14 illustrates the crack propagation paths following shear fracture of different Al-Cu-Mg laminated samples. The crack 
trajectories in PPLC samples primarily propagate along the interface between the intermediate layer and the Al-Cu-Mg alloy, showing a 
straight-line fracture morphology ( Fig. 14 (a)). However, when the number of ML particle structures is excessively high, shear cracks in 
the 1–1MPLC sample initiate around pores formed by the aggregation of ML particle structures, with fractures occurring in the central 
region of the intermediate layer ( Fig. 14 (b)). The shift in fracture location from the interface to the central region suggests that the 
wavy interface creates a mechanical interlocking effect, enhancing interfacial bonding strength ( Fig. 5 ).
A comparison of the shear fracture positions between the PPLC and 1–1MPLC samples shows that the wavy interlocking interface

FEM is an essential tool widely used to analyze crack propagation behavior, predict the crack growth path and fracture mode of 
materials under loading, and thereby reflect interfacial bonding strength ( Jin et al., 2024 ; Ran et al., 2024 ; Shen et al., 2025 ; Zhang

and the SiC model, to simulate shear tests under different conditions, as shown in Fig. 15 . During the loading process, crack propa -
gation in the non-SiC model begins at the interface between the Al-Cu-Mg alloy and the ZnAl₃ alloy layer, with the interface acting as 
the crack initiation source. After the load is applied, the simulation results show that the shear fracture mode of this model exhibits a 
straight fracture characteristic ( Fig. 15 (a)). This straight fracture mode is typically caused by a stress concentration at the interface, 
where the interfacial bonding strength is insufficient to resist further crack propagation, leading to uniform crack extension along the 
interface. In contrast, the crack propagation path in the SiC model is significantly influenced by the intermediate region of the 
ZnAl₃/SiC layer. During loading, the presence of SiC particles effectively alters the crack propagation path, resulting in a deflected 
fracture mode where the crack either follows or bypasses the particles ( Fig. 15 (b)). This deflected fracture mode indicates that the 
composite structure of SiC particles and ZnAl₃ powder can significantly alter shear crack propagation, thereby enhancing the material's 
resistance to crack growth. Compared to the non-SiC model, the SiC model exhibits more complex crack-propagation behavior,

propagation. To further compare the crack-propagation characteristics of the two FEM models, an image-based semi-quantitative

3.3.2. Micro-crack deflection mechanism
When the crack propagates along the ML particles interface or bypasses the ML particles, the crack path is shown to propagate along 
the boundary of the Zn-Al eutectoid precipitation zone and the non-precipitation zone ( Fig. 14 ). Therefore, the MCFL structure 
significantly influences crack propagation, warranting further analysis of its effect on crack behavior and the bonding strength 
mechanism.
Grain boundaries are among the main obstacles to crack propagation by hindering dislocation slip ( Ji et al., 2024 ; Zhang et al., 
2024 ). Fig. 16 shows SEM and EBSD images of the shear fracture in various regions of the 3 – 1MPLC sample. When shear fracture 
occurs in the 3 – 1MPLC samples, the main crack propagates along a reciprocating, tortuous path because the presence of “ ML ” particles 
continuously hinders and deflects its propagation. At the same time, several microcracks with different shapes are observed near the 
fracture ( Fig. 16 (a)). These microcracks mainly propagate in two ways ( Fig. 16 (b)): 
(I) The CFL-dominated bimodal grain-domain structure consists primarily of coarse and fine grains. During the shearing process, 
the crack starts from the outside of the CFL-dominated domain (fine-grained region), and it gradually extends to the edge of the 
CFL-dominated domain (the boundary of coarse-grained and fine-grained regions) ( Fig. 16 (c)): This crack propagation path 
results from significant differences in grain size between the coarse- and fine-grained regions. The low dislocation density in the 
coarse-grained region and the high dislocation density in the fine-grained region create a non-uniform dislocation density at the 
boundary ( Fig. 16 (d)), thereby generating a stress gradient. The stress gradient difference leads to a localized stress concen -
tration, which becomes the primary pathway for crack propagation ( Bahl et al., 2024 ; Jiang et al., 2024 ; Roth et al., 2025 ).
(II) The cracks initiate and continuously propagate outside of the CFL-dominated domain region, forming reciprocating deflections 
( Fig. 16 (e)): This crack propagation pattern arises due to the smaller grain size and closer spacing between grains, resulting in 
less hindrance from grain boundaries to crack propagation. Cracks are more likely to traverse through fine-grained regions. 
Additionally, grains with lower Schmid factors have difficulty activating slip systems during deformation, and crack propa -
gation in the crack-tip region relies on dislocation slip within the grains ( Lu et al., 2023 ; Pei et al., 2023 ). Therefore, when cracks 
encounter grains with different Schmid factor values, they are more likely to propagate towards the softer grains (higher Schmid 
factor values) ( Lu et al., 2023 ), resulting in crack deflection and reciprocation ( Fig. 16 (f)). These two forms of crack propagation 
enhance the resistance to crack propagation by increasing the path of crack propagation, thereby reducing the rate of crack 
propagation and increasing the interfacial bonding strength of the 3 – 1MPLC sample.

Fig. 18 illustrates the evolution of normalized strain energy distribution in the CFL-dominated grain-domain structure under

promotes energy dissipation during fracture, and thus contributes to the improvement of interfacial shear strength.

Overall, the MCFL structure enhances interfacial bonding by regulating crack propagation and increasing energy dissipation: (1) 
Macroscopic “ zigzag ” propagation: Unlike linear crack growth along traditional flat interfaces, ML hard constituents block direct crack 
paths, forcing cracks to bypass ML particles or propagate along ML/CFL interfaces, thereby forming a tortuous trajectory. (2) 
Microscopic coarse-fine grain deflection: Bimodal grains (1 – 2 μ m fine grains, 7 – 8 μ m coarse grains) from the “ CFL ” structure induce 
stress gradient and dislocation density differences; cracks initiate in fine grains, deflect reciprocally at coarse-fine grain boundaries due 
to stress concentration, and further deflect toward slip-prone soft grains Therefore, when cracks encounter grains with different 
Schmid factor values, they are more likely to propagate toward grains with higher Schmid factors, where slip systems are more readily 
activated. This promotes crack deflection and reciprocating crack propagation, thereby increasing crack-path tortuosity and crack- 
propagation resistance. Synergistic regulation of these two mechanisms greatly improves the interfacial bonding strength of Al 
laminated alloys.
Therefore, through the triple synergy of the MCFL structure, the wavy interlocking interface, and the bimodal grain structure, the 
interfacial system of Al-laminated alloys realizes a multi-scale crack-deflection strengthening mechanism ( Fig. 20 ). The first one 
originates from the stress field gradient generated by the MCFL structure, forcing the crack path to deflect multiple times; the second 
one relies on the three-dimensional morphological characteristics of the wavy interlocking interface to extend the crack propagation 
path through the mechanical interlocking effect; The third one can be attributed to the non-uniform deformation characteristics of the 
bimodal grain structure, forming a strain gradient between the fine grain zone and the coarse grain zone, which further consumes the 
crack propagation energy. The interfacial bonding strength can be significantly improved by a multi-level synergistic strengthening 
mechanism, which also provides a vital interface design direction for the development of new high-performance layered composites 
and opens new avenues for deeper understanding of the interfacial strengthening and toughening mechanisms in heterogeneous 
materials.
4. Conclusion
The wavy interlocking interface and MCFL structure of Al-Cu-Mg laminated alloys were first achieved through interlayer design, 
and the mechanism underlying their influence on interface bonding strength was identified. The following conclusions can thus be 
drawn: 
(1) Al-Cu-Mg laminated alloys with high interfacial bonding strength were successfully obtained through MCFL-inspired interlayer 
design. The optimized 3 – 1MPLC sample exhibited an interfacial shear strength of 52.57 MPa, which was 44.46% higher than 
that of the corresponding PPLC sample. Meanwhile, the fracture behavior changed from a flat/straight interfacial fracture mode 
to a zigzag fracture mode, indicating enhanced crack-path regulation capability.
(2) The MCFL-inspired interlayer was constructed by combining a wavy interlocking interface with a mineral-like/collagen fiber- 
like hard-soft architecture. The wavy interlocking interface was generated by the non-uniform compression between the soft 
pure Al layer and mineral-like (ML) hard constituents during hot rolling. In addition, the non-uniform precipitation of the Zn-Al 
eutectoid phase hindered grain growth and promoted the formation of collagen fiber-like (CFL) domain structures in the 
interlayer.
(3) The wavy interlocking interface and ML structure mainly contributed to interfacial strengthening through mechanical locking 
and crack-path deflection. Experimental fracture observations showed that the uniformly distributed ML particles promoted 
crack bypassing and crack branching. FEM results further revealed that the wavy interface and ML particles induced local stress 
redistribution, increased the resistance to direct interfacial crack propagation, and thereby improved the interfacial bonding 
strength.
(4) The CFL domain structure further enhanced fracture resistance by regulating local crack propagation within the interlayer. 
Experimental observations showed that cracks underwent repeated local deflection within the CFL-related domains. CP-PF 
simulations further demonstrated that the heterogeneity in strain energy, stress gradient, and grain orientation promoted 
crack initiation, crack deflection, and secondary crack activation. These experimental and modeling results jointly confirm that 
the enhanced bonding strength originates from the cooperative effects of crack-path tortuosity and energy dissipation.
