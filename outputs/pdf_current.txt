ABSTRACT
Electropulsing-assisted aging (EAA) has emerged as a promising route for modifying the micro -
structure and mechanical properties of thin-walled superalloy components. However, the re -
lationships among current parameters, γ ″ precipitate evolution, and mechanical response remain 
insufficiently understood. In this study, EAA and uniaxial tensile tests were performed on GH4169 
sheet specimens. An electromigration-informed precipitate evolution model was established 
based on TEM observations, and a crystal plasticity finite element model (CPFEM) incorporating 
precipitate shearing and Orowan bypassing resistances was then developed, with a multi-phase 
representative volume element (RVE) model. The constitutive framework was implemented in 
Abaqus using VUMAT. Parameters were identified via inverse FE calibration with the particle 
swarm optimization (PSO) method. The results indicate a temperature-driven enhancement of 
electromigration during the γ ″ coarsening process and a strengthening mechanism based on multi- 
mode shearing and bypassing. These findings provide a mechanistic basis for describing the EAA- 
modified tensile response of GH4169 and offer useful insight for future EAA-assisted processing of 
thin-walled superalloy components.
1. Introduction
Owing to its outstanding properties of high strength and good oxidation/corrosion resistance, GH4169 has been widely used in 
critical high-temperature components of aero-engines ( Gudivada and Pandey, 2023 ). As next-generation hypersonic engines evolve 
toward much more compact architectures and higher power densities, thin-walled GH4169 microtubes have been increasingly adopted 
in compact heat exchangers, as well as other micro-scale structures ( Meng et al., 2021 ; Chen et al., 2022 ). To meet the forming re -
quirements for such thin-walled components, Gao et al. (2022) investigated electrically assisted forming (EAF) and showed that it can 
improve formability. However, applying pulsed current during forming increases process complexity and makes it difficult to ensure 
sufficient strength and stability. Wang et al. (2022) showed that EAA can be used as an offline treatment before forming. This strategy 
can regulate strengthening behavior and plasticity within a short treatment time without increasing process complexity. However, 
EAA introduces mechanisms that differ from those in EAF. Traditional constitutive models cannot accurately predict the mechanical 
properties after EAA. Therefore, this study used the CPFEM method to describe the mechanical behavior of GH4169 after EAA, 
building on the authors ’ previous modeling work ( Tian et al., 2023 ).

and the electron-wind effect can significantly alter material diffusion and phase transformation behavior. Hui et al. (2025) showed that 
pulsed current and aging time jointly influence atomic diffusion and phase transformation kinetics. As a result, the size and distribution 
of precipitates change, which further affects the strength and plasticity of the material. In addition, Gao et al. (2022) showed that 
electric current can affect deformation behavior by changing slip-system activity and grain rotation. It can also modify the effective 
barriers for APB/stacking-fault (SF) formation. As a result, the hierarchy and cooperativity of multi-slip deformation may change and 
further affect the overall mechanical behavior of the alloy. However, the coupled relationship between microstructural evolution and 
multi-slip deformation under EAA remains poorly understood, especially in GH4169, where quantitative validation is still limited.
Quantification of precipitates is essential for understanding the macroscopic properties of materials. Precipitate evolution pro -
cesses under EAA exhibit similarities to those during conventional thermal aging, because the underlying physics are governed by 
solute diffusion and phase-transformation kinetics. Therefore, current-related effects can be incorporated into classical precipitate 
evolution theories to model and predict precipitate coarsening under EAA conditions ( Deschamps and Hutchinson, 2021 ). Developing 
a precipitate-evolution-based model for EAA requires careful consideration of the size and distribution of the coarsening γ ″ pre -
cipitates. Xiang and Zhang (2019) suggested that electron force and electron-wind effects can influence the evolution of dislocation 
and defect structures, thereby promoting microstructural evolution by affecting diffusion kinetics. In addition, Xiao et al. (2019)
proposed a model incorporating electromigration effects with reasonable accuracy. However, it has not been quantitatively calibrated 
or validated against precipitate observations such as size or volume fraction, which limits its applicability to further prediction.
Although the above precipitate-evolution studies can quantify EAA-induced changes in γ ″ size, distribution, and coarsening ki -
netics, a crystal-scale constitutive framework is still required to translate these evolving precipitate characteristics into slip resistance, 
phase-dependent plastic flow, and macroscopic mechanical response. CPFEM provides such a bridge between microstructural evo -
lution and deformation mechanisms at the slip-system and grain scales. Representative studies have used CPFEM to describe yield- 
locus evolution with dislocation-density hardening and anisotropic elasticity, length-scale-informed plasticity, multiaxial ratcheting 
with microstructural effects, and strength evolution under complex stress states ( Iftikhar et al., 2022 ; Chatterjee et al., 2025 ; Li et al., 
2022 ; Zheng and Yoon, 2024 ). In precipitation-hardened superalloys, microstructural evolution after EAA can be linked to macro -
scopic strength through crystal-scale deformation mechanisms. Extensive research has therefore been devoted to modeling precipitate 
strengthening mechanisms ( Cui et al., 2024 ). Gupta and Bronkhorst (2021) highlighted the fundamental differences in intra-phase slip 
dynamics in nickel-based superalloys and proposed separate flow rules for the γ and γ ′ phases within a dislocation-density-based crystal 
plasticity framework. Zhang et al. (2024) further proposed a constitutive model for nickel-based superalloys that incorporates 
deformation mechanisms such as APB shearing and micro-twinning, thereby providing a more physically based description of 
deformation and hardening. For multiphase and polycrystalline systems, the EPSC model developed by Ghorbanpour et al. (2017)
attempted to incorporate precipitation-hardening effects within a homogeneous framework. However, its characterization of key 
microstructural features, such as precipitate size and morphology, remains limited. More recently, Li et al. (2020) embedded 
precipitation-hardening constitutive laws into numerical crystal-plasticity formulations and predicted constitutive responses by 
combining the precipitate-matrix orientation relationship with precipitate morphology. At present, RVE modeling based on CPFEM 
and homogenization theory has been widely applied to evaluate the statistically averaged response of multiphase alloys ( Cai et al., 
2020 ; Mikula et al., 2024 ). However, many existing models still employ equivalent or single flow rules to describe multiphase 
deformation ( Guo et al., 2025 ), making it difficult to simultaneously capture the independent dynamics of different phases, the 
activation of multiple slip systems, and the interactions between dislocations and precipitates. Consequently, it is necessary to develop 
a CP model considering these coupled effects under EAA conditions.
In this study, an electromigration-informed precipitate evolution model was established to quantitatively describe microstructural 
changes, and a crystal plasticity constitutive model was developed. A multiphase RVE framework was subsequently constructed, and 
the constitutive model was implemented in Abaqus via VUMAT for crystal-scale simulations. Model parameters were calibrated 
through inverse FE identification coupled with PSO, and the resulting model demonstrated good predictive accuracy under different 
EAA conditions. Compared with existing CPFEM or precipitation-hardening models, the present framework links EAA-dependent γ ″ 
coarsening with a two-phase γ / γ ″ CPFEM, so that the current-dependent precipitate size can be transferred into the competing shearing 
and Orowan resistances. This enables the post-EAA tensile response of GH4169 to be predicted from γ ″ precipitate evolution within the

processing of thin-walled superalloy components.
2. Materials and methods
2.1. Experimental procedure
2.1.1. Material and specimen preparation
GH4169 sheet with a thickness of 1.0mm was selected as the experimental material, and its chemical composition is shown in 
Table 1 .
The tensile specimens were designed according to ASTM E8/E8M-08. Specimens were obtained by wire cutting along the RD 
(rolling direction) of the GH4169 sheet, with their geometric dimensions shown in Fig. 1 .
GH4169 is a precipitation-hardening nickel-based superalloy requiring solution treatment before aging. The specimens were coated 
with an anti-oxidation coating and then solution treated at 1020
◦
C for 20min in a vacuum furnace, followed by water quenching to 
room temperature. The material after solution treatment is referred to as the solution-treated condition (ST).
Subsequently, the specimens were subjected to EAA on a pulse current-assisted vacuum heat treatment platform. This platform 
primarily comprises a vacuum maintenance system, a power supply, a clamping system, and a temperature measurement system, as 
illustrated in Fig. 2 . To ensure electrical and thermal conductivity, copper alloy was selected for the specimen clamps, which were 
connected to the specimens inside the vacuum chamber. To maintain constant current during aging, the pulse power supply employed 
constant current control. To ensure the accuracy of non-contact temperature measurement, an infrared thermal imaging camera was 
used to measure temperatures through the barium fluoride window on the vacuum furnace. The infrared temperature measurement 
was calibrated against a Type K thermocouple to obtain accurate aging temperatures.
To obtain different precipitate sizes, the current settings for EAA are listed in Table 2 , while the solution treatment and EAA process 
parameters are summarized in Fig. 3 . In this study, the pulsed aging current was set to 5, 10, 11, 12, 15, and 20 A, corresponding to 
peak current densities J of 16.7, 33.3, 36.7, 40.0, 50.0, and 66.7 A/mm², respectively. In addition, an intermediate 11 A condition ( J =
36.7 A/mm², steady aging temperature = 605.3
◦
C) was introduced as a supplementary state for TEM observation and for tensile-model 
validation within the precipitation strengthening window. The high-frequency pulsed current had a duty cycle of 1:19 and a period of 
0.001 s. EAA was conducted for 0.5, 1.0, and 2.0 h, and the treated material was termed the aged state (AS). Upon current application, 
the specimen heating rate reached up to approximately 90
◦
C/s. The temperature was primarily governed by Joule heating and rapidly 
stabilized. As shown in Fig. 3 , the temperature distribution within the gauge section during EAA was relatively uniform, with the center 
slightly warmer than the ends; however, the maximum deviation from the set aging temperature was less than 10
◦
C, meeting the 
temperature homogeneity requirement.
2.1.2. Uniaxial tensile test
To quantitatively investigate changes in mechanical properties following different EAA treatments, specimens underwent uniaxial 
tensile tests on an MTS E44.204 universal testing machine. The machine's maximum test force was 20 kN, with a maximum crosshead 
travel of 1150 mm. Tensile tests were conducted at room temperature with a tensile rate of 25 μ m/s, corresponding to a strain rate of 
0.001 s ⁻ ¹. Each condition was tested three times to ensure experimental accuracy. True strain during the tensile process was measured 
using digital image correlation (DIC).
The true stress – strain curves of specimens under different current and aging times are shown in Fig. 4 . It should be noted that due to 
the application of an initial preload of 25 N during the tensile test, the true stress at the onset of the curve was not zero. Fig. 4 (a), (d), 
and (e) show that when the current was set to 5, 15, and 20 A, the mechanical response of the specimens was essentially consistent with 
that of ST, showing no significant strengthening effect. This indicated that the corresponding equivalent temperatures lay outside the 
precipitation window of the alloy's primary strengthening phases. In contrast, when the current was within the range of 10 – 12 A, the 
specimens exhibited a significant strengthening response, and the strengthening effect further intensified with increasing aging time. 
Notably, the equivalent temperature range corresponding to this strengthening interval fell within the reported precipitation tem -
perature range for typical strengthening phases γ ′ / γ ″ in GH4169 ( Lin et al., 2017 ). Therefore, the observed improvement in mechanical 
properties under EAA conditions can be preliminarily attributed to precipitation strengthening.

2.2. Numerical implementation
2.2.1. Multiphase RVE model
To analyze deformation and strengthening mechanisms based on crystal plasticity theory, this study constructed a polycrystalline 
RVE model. Using Abaqus Python scripts, five polycrystalline RVEs were generated with 2744 C3D8R elements (14 × 14 × 14), and 
their results were statistically averaged. The grain structure was generated using a three-dimensional Voronoi algorithm with 38 
randomly distributed grain seeds. Based on the weak crystallographic texture indicated by the EBSD results, which supports the near- 
random orientation assumption, each grain was assigned a random crystal orientation to construct the polycrystalline RVE model.

Within the aforementioned crystal plasticity analysis framework, to quantitatively characterize the influence of γ ″ precipitates on 
the deformation behavior of the γ matrix in numerical simulations, γ ″ precipitate elements were further introduced into the poly -
crystalline RVE. This established a two-phase RVE model composed of the FCC γ matrix phase and the D0 ₂₂ -structured γ ″ precipitate 
phase, as shown in the RVE part of Fig. 10 . To reflect the material's multiphase characteristics, two partially independent sets of 
material parameters were assigned to the γ matrix phase and the γ ″ precipitate, respectively. The influence of this random γ ″ distri -
bution on the macroscopic response was evaluated by additional RVE realizations with different precipitate arrangements, and only 
limited scatter in the macroscopic stress – strain response was observed. This modeling strategy focused on revealing the dominant role 
of precipitation-related strengthening behavior under ideal polycrystalline conditions. Therefore, macroscopic defect factors such as 
voids and cracks were not explicitly introduced, while the actual size ratio and complex geometric morphology of the precipitates 
observed in experiments were not fully reproduced. Based on these assumptions, the number of γ ″ elements was used as a numerical 
descriptor to represent different precipitate states under various EAA conditions.
Based on the microstructural observations, the variation in the γ ″ fraction among the investigated EAA conditions was limited 
compared with the changes in γ ″ size. This behavior is consistent with Ostwald ripening ( Liu et al., 2024 ), in which smaller precipitates 
are consumed by larger ones during coarsening and the total precipitate fraction does not necessarily increase proportionally with 
particle size. In addition, Wang et al. (2013) reported that the γ ″ volume fraction in IN718 remained approximately stable during 
electric-field treatment over a much longer aging duration, while precipitate coarsening was evident. Therefore, this model neglected 
the evolution of γ ″ volume fraction and instead used precipitate size as the primary parameter to characterize the strengthening 
response.
2.2.2. Simulation procedure
For the constructed multiphase crystal RVE model, the loading direction was aligned with the RD, while the other two directions 
were subjected to symmetric constraints to prevent rigid-body motion. Based on the experimental results described above, the overall 
scheme of the present study is summarized in Fig. 10 . TEM observations of γ ″ precipitates provided the key microstructural descriptors 
used in the present study, primarily the average precipitate size and its qualitative spatial distribution under different EAA conditions. 
These experimental results were used on the one hand to construct the multiphase RVE model and on the other hand to calibrate the 
microstructural evolution model in the time – current space. The predicted precipitate state was then introduced into the crystal 
plasticity constitutive framework as a microstructural input. The RVE served as the numerical framework for the Abaqus imple -
mentation. Because γ ″ precipitates were randomly distributed in the RVE, lattice mismatch and interfacial stress concentrations arose 
between the γ ″ precipitates and the matrix. Therefore, the model was implemented in Abaqus using a VUMAT subroutine to improve 
numerical stability and avoid numerical difficulties caused by stress discontinuities. Specifically, the crystal plasticity model employed 
in Abaqus was based on an existing UMAT framework and was reimplemented as a VUMAT subroutine for the Abaqus Explicit solver. 
Incremental updates were performed using an Euler explicit integration scheme, thereby enhancing the overall simulation stability and 
convergence efficiency ( Asadkandi et al., 2025 ). This workflow established a closed link between EAA-induced precipitate evolution 
and the corresponding precipitation-strengthened mechanical response.
Within this overall scheme, two key issues remain to be clarified. First, existing studies generally suggest that significant precip -
itation of γ ″ requires a long aging time, whereas pronounced precipitates were revealed within a short time in the EAA process. 
Therefore, the evolution mechanism needs to be further clarified. Second, although experiments have confirmed the correlation be -
tween precipitates and improved mechanical properties, the influence of precipitate-dislocation interactions on deformation resistance 
and plastic response remains unclear. The following sections address these two issues separately through the microstructure evolution 
and crystal plasticity framework. The present model was calibrated and validated for the relatively uniform gauge region of laboratory

4.2. Parameter calibration of the crystal plasticity constitutive model
The average γ ″ precipitate size obtained in Section 4.1 was used as a fixed input for the subsequent calibration. By fitting the 
simulated tensile stress-strain curves to the experimental results, a parameter inversion procedure was established using particle 
swarm optimization (PSO). The overall procedure is shown in Fig. 14 .
As illustrated in Fig. 14 , a multi-stage calibration procedure was adopted to obtain multi-phase CP model parameters applicable to 
the ST and multiple EAA conditions. The calibration procedure was organized into two steps. Stage I and Stage II were first combined as 
Step 1 to obtain a parameter set that satisfies both ST and the 10A-1H condition. This parameter set was then used as input for Step 2 
(Stage III), where its performance under the remaining EAA conditions was evaluated based on E
total
. If E
total 
exceeded the prescribed 
criterion, the procedure returned to Step 1; otherwise, the parameter set was accepted as the final result.
In Stage I, an RVE containing only the γ matrix was constructed to represent the ST condition, and the uniaxial tensile curve of ST 
was taken as the target for identifying the shared parameters and γ matrix parameters through PSO. In this way, a basic parameter set 
capable of describing the matrix-dominated flow behavior was first established.
In Stage II, the γ ″ precipitate size corresponding to the 10A-1H condition was introduced into the two-phase RVE. Based on the basic 
parameter set identified in Stage I, the γ ″ precipitate parameters were further calibrated by PSO using the tensile response under the 
10A-1H condition. This stage resulted in a parameter set simultaneously applicable to both ST and 10A-1H, which was then used as the 
initial parameter set for Stage III.
In Stage III, the tensile curves for multiple AS conditions (10A-30min, 12A-30min, and 12A-1H) were further incorporated into a 
weighted error function. The corresponding RVEs for each condition were considered within the same PSO framework. Each particle 
was evaluated using E
total 
and a predefined error criterion. The resulting parameter set was obtained for all investigated EAA con -
ditions.

4.3. Model validation
To validate the predictive capability of the γ ″ -evolution-informed CPFEM, the 11A – 30min condition was first used as an 
interpolation-type validation case because it lies between the calibrated 10A and 12A states. To further examine the model outside the 
original calibrated aging-duration range, an additional 11.7A – 10min condition was selected. This condition remains within the 
precipitation-strengthening current range, whereas its aging duration is shorter than the 30min – 1H duration range used in the main 
calibration dataset. For both validation conditions, the γ ″ size was predicted from the precipitate-evolution model and then introduced 
into the CPFEM without recalibrating the constitutive parameters. As shown in Fig. 15 (b), the predicted stress – strain responses show 
reasonable agreement with the experimental curves, supporting the predictive capability of the model within and near the investigated 
EAA strengthening window.

5.1. Temperature-driven enhancement of electromigration during the γ ″ coarsening process
The coarsening of γ ″ precipitates during EAA is influenced by both thermally activated solute diffusion and electromigration effects. 
As shown in Fig. 16 (a), the contributions of thermal diffusion and electromigration vary with the γ ″ precipitation temperature. The

thereby further accelerating precipitate coarsening.

5.2. Strengthening mechanism based on multi-mode shearing and bypassing
Fig. 17 shows a pronounced non-monotonic trend in both yield strength and elongation under EAA conditions. As the effective 
current increases, the yield strength in Fig. 17 (a) first rises and then falls after a distinct inflection point, whereas the elongation in 
Fig. 17 (b) exhibits the opposite trend. Simultaneously, this inflection point moves toward a lower current's direction with an increasing 
aging time. These results indicate that the mechanical response is governed by the coupled effects of current and aging time.
As shown in Fig. 17 (a), noticeable strengthening occurs only within a limited current range. In the present study, the average γ ″ 
precipitate size, r , is taken as the dominant microstructural parameter controlling strengthening, allowing different EAA conditions to 
be represented by different r values. In general, dislocation – precipitate interactions are controlled by two competing modes, pre -
cipitate shearing and Orowan bypassing. τ

the overall strengthening effect is governed by the weighting of shearing and bypassing mechanisms. Specifically, within this 
strengthening regime, multiple dislocation-precipitate interaction mechanisms act synergistically to contribute to the overall 
strengthening, leading to a higher macroscopic yield strength. By contrast, once the precipitate size falls outside this strengthening 
region, only one interaction mechanism tends to dominate, and the strengthening effect is correspondingly weakened. Specifically, 
when r falls below the strengthening region, τ
α
shear 
is relatively low whereas τ
or 
remains high, so dislocations tend to shear γ ″ precipitates 
and leave the corresponding SFs. In contrast, when r exceeds the strengthening region, bypassing becomes dominant and dislocation 
motion proceeds mainly by looping around the precipitates. As shown in Fig. 18 (b), both shearing and bypassing features were 
observed around γ ″ precipitates, providing microstructural support for the above interpretation. Thus, dominance of only one inter -
action mode leads to a non-uniform distribution of microscopic resistance, resulting in weaker yield strengthening. Accordingly, EAA 
can modify the relative contribution of shearing and bypassing by controlling γ ″ precipitate size, thereby providing mechanistic insight 
for subsequent process optimization.
The precipitate shearing can be further resolved into five shearing modes, as illustrated in Fig. 19 . These modes generate distinct SF 
configurations, including APB/APB-like faults and SF variants such as ISF, SISF, and CSF, which are generated by shearing of different 
displacement vectors in the D0 ₂₂ γ ″ structure, as illustrated by Lv et al. (2016) . The non-uniform activation of these shear modes may 
affect the mechanical response in two ways. First, it may promote slip-system biasing, so that plastic activity tends to concentrate on 
only a limited number of slip paths. Second, it can cause plastic-strain partitioning, with strain distributed unevenly among different 
paths. This may reduce deformation compatibility and promote local strain accumulation. Taken together, precipitate shearing is 
strongly path-dependent: each slip path corresponds to a specific shear mode and must overcome its own formation barrier. Slip paths 
with lower barriers are therefore preferentially activated, whereas those with higher barriers contribute less. To quantify this rela -
tionship, the slip-path activation weight A
α 
obtained from PSO inversion is summarized in Table 7 . Here, A
α 
should be interpreted as an 
effective path-activation weight with partial physical meaning, rather than as an independently measurable physical fraction of slip 
events. In particular, the 1/6 [ 11 2 ] slip system exhibits a much larger A
α 
value than the other paths, whereas the other paths have 
similarly lower weights. This non-uniform distribution is consistent with the large differences in SF energies reported by Lv et al. 
(2016) . Accordingly, plastic deformation tends to concentrate on slip paths with larger A
α 
values, which simultaneously increases 
yield strength and the early-stage hardening exponent. In addition, the same concentration may reduce compatibility among slip paths 
and limit cooperative deformation, thereby promoting local strain accumulation.
6. Conclusions
This study established a γ ″ precipitate-evolution-informed CPFEM describing the mechanical behavior of GH4169 under EAA. A 
coupled framework integrating precipitate microevolution, the CP model, and the polycrystalline RVE model was established. The 
main conclusions are as follows: 
(1) A γ ″ precipitate microevolution model considering electromigration effects and a crystal plasticity constitutive model consid -
ering precipitate shearing and bypassing were established and implemented in Abaqus via VUMAT, with a two-phase RVE 
model. The microevolution parameters were calibrated from TEM statistical data, and the constitutive parameters were

(2) During EAA, the γ ″ coarsening behavior was governed by thermal diffusion at lower temperatures and by electromigration at

(3) A pronounced non-monotonic trend was shown in both yield strength and elongation. Strengthening was closely associated with 
the precipitate size r . The strengthening region was established and found to coincide with the overlap region of shearing and 
bypassing. Outside this region, a single interaction mechanism was dominant and weakened the strengthening effect. The non- 
uniform activation of the five shearing modes was quantitatively confirmed, leading to path-dependent shearing, slip-system 
biasing, and local strain accumulation.
