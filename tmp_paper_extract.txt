ABSTRACT
Overcoming the strength-ductility trade-off in additively manufactured 9Cr oxide dispersion 
strengthened (ODS) alloys is a significant challenge in structural materials design. In this work, 
multi-heterogeneous microstructures are orchestrated to overcome the strength-ductility trade-off 
by employing Y
6
WO
12
@W (YWO@W) nanoscale core-shell precursor. The multi-heterogeneous 
structures comprise YWO@W/YWO nanoparticles (NPs), bimodal grains, heterogeneous de -
fects, and heterogeneous deformation zones. The W shell alleviates the density mismatch between 
YWO NPs and molten matrix, thereby suppressing YWO NPs flotation during additive 
manufacturing. Concurrently, partial dissolution of W triggers asynchronous martensitic trans -
formations and forms heterogeneous defect zones. The heterogeneous structure interfaces 
generate substantial geometrically necessary dislocations, resulting in a back stress strengthening 
of 357 MPa. The uniformly dispersed ~25 nm YWO/YWO@W NPs provide a contribution of 123 
MPa from Orowan strengthening. Quantitative strengthening modeling incorporating Orowan, 
grain boundary, dislocation, solid solution and back stress contributions predicts a yield strength 
of 880 MPa, consistent with experimental measurements. During plastic deformation, the low- 
defect and deformation zones within the heterogeneous structures facilitate dislocation storage, 
while the YWO/YWO@W NPs promote dislocation cross-slip. The non-coherent interface between 
YWO NPs and the matrix may induce dislocation slip along the {1 2 1} < 1 1 1 > system, which in 
turn enables multi-slip systems to operate simultaneously. Finally, the YWO@W sample exhibits 
tensile strength of 1214 MPa and elongation of 10.3%, representing increases of 45.6% and 
145.2%, respectively, compared to Y
2
O
3 
sample. This work offers a novel strategy to overcome 
the strength-ductility trade-off in additively manufactured ODS alloys.

The inherent differences in physical properties — such as density — between the metal and oxide phases cause the oxide particles to 
float and agglomerate in the melt pool during AM ( Chang et al., 2015 ; Wilms et al., 2023 ). This buoyancy-induced aggregation poses a 
significant barrier to homogeneous dispersion of nano-oxides in matrix. As a result, the expected dispersion strengthening effect is 
undermined, and the oxide clusters act as stress concentrators for crack initiation, which severely degrades the overall mechanical 
performance ( Wilms et al., 2023 ). The in-situ dispersion method represents a highly promising strategy for fabricating AM ODS alloys 
( Ma et al., 2026 ; Zhao et al., 2024 ). However, its primary constraint lies in achieving uniform oxidation, a challenge critically governed 
by matrix composition. Reactive elements such as Al tend to form detrimental, coarse Y – Al – O or Al
2
O
3 
phases, which compromise 
ductility. Furthermore, the transient nature and ultra-high cooling rates inherent to the millisecond-scale molten pool impede ho -
mogeneous oxide distribution ( Zheng et al., 2025 ), ultimately diminishing the Orowan dispersion strengthening effect. Therefore, The 
AM process of ODS alloys faces significant challenges.
At the same time, the high residual stresses generated from non-uniform thermal contraction during the severe thermal cycles 
( Doumenc et al., 2024 ; Niu et al., 2024 ; Tekumalla et al., 2024 ), along with the significant lattice strain at the coarse oxide-matrix 
interfaces ( Dong et al., 2021 ), will further reduce the ductility of the additively manufactured ODS alloy ( Sohail et al., 2024 ). 
Thermal treatments, such as hot isostatic pressing (HIP), are commonly employed to mitigate the high residual stresses inherent in 
printed alloys ( Li et al., 2025a ; Shi et al., 2019 ; Smith et al., 2023 ). However, achieving high ductility through this approach often 
comes at the cost of sacrificing the dislocation strengthening effect ( Lee et al., 2020 ). For 9Cr martensitic ODS steels, HIP also carries 
the risk of destabilizing the martensitic structure, promoting its transformation to ferrite ( Li et al., 2025a ; Shi et al., 2019 ; Yang et al., 
2026 ). In recent years, researchers have successfully engineered multi-heterogeneous structure in different alloys through various 
strategies, including compositional design ( An et al., 2024 ), multi-stage heat treatments ( Wang et al., 2024 ), and alterations in AM 
parameters ( Kim et al., 2024 ; Lv et al., 2025 ). Heterogeneous structure enhances material strength by creating deformation in -
compatibility between the "soft zones" and "hard zones" within them, which generates strong back stress and additional work hard -
ening at the interfaces ( Chu et al., 2024 ; Peng et al., 2025 ). Simultaneously, this structure enables plastic deformation to progressively 
spread from the soft zones to the hard zones, avoiding deformation localization, thereby maintaining good ductility even while 
achieving high strength ( Li et al., 2023 ; Shen et al., 2025 ). Although heterogeneous structures help overcome the strength-ductility 
trade-off. Introducing a multi-level heterogeneous structure incorporating heterogeneous oxide dispersoids into additively manu -
factured 9Cr ODS steel presents a significant challenge. Firstly, the buoyancy-driven floating of oxides within the molten pool prevents 
their uniform distribution throughout the matrix, thereby inhibiting the formation of abundant hetero-interfaces between the oxides 
and the matrix ( Wilms et al., 2023 ). This undermines the synergistic strengthening effects of Orowan looping and back stress. 
Additionally, the coarse agglomerates formed by these oxides induce severe stress concentration, leading to a deterioration in ductility 
( Wilms et al., 2023 ). Furthermore, the inherently high cooling rates of DED induce a uniform martensitic transformation, while 
suppressing the full precipitation of secondary phases, thereby inhibiting the formation of matrix heterogeneous structures.
To address this, we employ a YWO@W precursor design strategy. This approach achieves the uniform distribution of nano-oxides 
within the crystal lattice, establishing abundant oxide-matrix hetero-interfaces while simultaneously forming a coarse/fine-grained 
heterogeneous structure. Moreover, leveraging the characteristic of incomplete dissolution of W during Directed Energy Deposition 
(DED), we constructed high- and low-density defect zones. Ultimately, this multi-level heterogeneous structure overcome the strength- 
ductility trade-off of 9Cr ODS steels.

In the molten pool, the bonding force between the YWO core and the W shell is influenced by a combination of chemical bonds, 
thermal stress, and van der Waals force ( Li et al., 2004 ). The coherent interface formed between YWO and W shell in YWO@W 
precursor and matrix alloy ( Figs. 2 (e) and 5 (f)) revel that the thermal stress that drives W shell exfoliation is significantly reduced in 
the molten pool. At the same time, the YWO core and W shell are bound by both chemical bonds and van der Waals attraction, further 
reducing the segregation between YWO and W in the molten pool. Furthermore, the extremely short duration of the molten pool, 
combined with the sluggish diffusion coefficient of W in the matrix ( Fig. 1 (c)), further suppresses the dissolution of the W shell within 
the molten pool. The W shell — which combines a high melting point, a low diffusion coefficient in the matrix, and a coherent interface 
with YWO — maintains excellent structural stability in the molten pool, effectively impeding the agglomeration and coarsening of YWO 
NPs. Ultimately, the average size of the oxide nanoparticles distributed in the three-dimensional space of the YWO@W alloy is 25 nm 
( Fig. 7 (a,b)). Finely dispersed oxide nanoparticles provide excellent work-hardening capability by hindering dislocation motion at 
both room and elevated temperatures ( Xu et al., 2025 ).
However, during the rapid cooling and the thermal effects generated by the next printing pass process (predominantly solid phase), 
residual stress caused by the difference of coefficient of thermal expansion develops between the YWO core and the W shell ( Das et al., 
2014 ; Zhang et al., 2021 ), which directly prompts the detachment of W atoms from the shell layer and their subsequent dissolution into 
the matrix. Studies have shown that the diffusion rate of atoms of shell is faster on smaller core ( Ding et al., 2024 ), thereby leading to 
the faster dissolution of W shell on smaller YWO core within an extremely short time. The dissolution of W primarily occurs during the 
stage of molten pool solidification and the subsequent stage of high thermal input from other printing passes. Due to the short 
dissolution time and the slow diffusion rate of W in the matrix, compositional inhomogeneity may result.
It can be observed from Fig. 7 (c, d) that a marked reduction in the maximum, minimum, and average dimensions of the YWO@W/ 
YWO NPs has been achieved compared to other additively manufactured ODS alloys ( Wilms et al., 2023 ), in the absence of significant 
oxides coarsening (exceeding 200 nm). The uniform distribution of YWO@W/YWO NPs within the matrix indicates that the YWO@W 
core-shell structure plays a dual role by maintaining good structural stability during AM process: (1) W shell prevents the floating of 
YWO NPs, (2) the W shell provides effective protection against coarsening of YWO NPs.

Fig. 15 illustrates the deformation mechanism schematic of the YWO@W-2000 alloy. The matrix of the YWO@W-2000 alloy 
features a multi-heterogeneous structure, comprising coarse-grain/fine-grain (CG/FG) regions as well as high-/low-defect zones 
(HDZ/LDZ) within the coarse-grained regions. The high density of grain boundaries in these FG effectively pins dislocation motion. 
The formation of the HDZ/LDZ is due to the asynchronous transformation of martensite caused by the YWO@W precursor. The 
interwoven distribution of HDZ and LDZ within CG regions plays a critical role in enhancing the mechanical properties of the 
YWO@W-2000 alloy. The LDZ facilitates dislocation glide and storage under high loading conditions, thereby improving ductility. 
Conversely, the HDZ promotes dislocation entanglement, leading to enhanced strength. Furthermore, the alternating arrangement of 
HDZ and LDZ effectively mitigates local lattice strain and suppresses premature crack initiation. During deformation, a heterogeneous 
structure consisting of deformable and non-deformable zones (DZ and NDZ) forms. The low Schmid factor grains, formed under the 
influence of crystal orientation, are strongly pinned by YWO@W/YWO NPs, forming serrated grain boundaries, which ultimately leads 
to the formation of the NDZ. These NDZs enhance mechanical strength by impeding dislocation glide. Simultaneously, their serrated 
grain boundaries promote microcrack deflection, which inhibits the initiation of main cracks. During the initial deformation stage, 
dislocations within the DZ primarily undergo slip on the {1 1 0} < 1 1 1 > system. As deformation progresses, multiple slip systems may 
be activated simultaneously, specifically {1 1 0} < 1 1 1 > and {1 2 1} < 1 1 1 > , enhancing the ductility. Moreover, the heterogeneous 
microstructure interfaces generate substantial geometrically necessary dislocations, the back stress generated by dislocation pile-ups at 
the interfaces of these heterogeneous structures further impedes the continued motion of dislocations, thereby significantly enhancing 
the strength.
During the AM, YWO@W core-shell structure with matrix density matching is used as the precursor, which effectively prevents the 
flotation and agglomeration of oxides in the molten pool, ultimately achieving a uniform distribution of both YWO@W and YWO NPs 
in the matrix. This significantly enhanced the Orowan strengthening effect. For the YWO@W NPs, due to the very thin W shell (1 – 2 nm,

During deformation, when encounter YWO@W and YWO NPs, dislocations tend to multiplication via Frank-Read sources, entan -
glement, and ultimately the formation of dislocation walls and cells. Moreover, the interaction of dislocations with YWO@W and YWO 
NPs induces cross-slip. During the later stages of deformation, the YWO NPs may promote dislocation slip on the {1 2 1} < 1 1 1 >
system by altering their non-coherent interface relationship with the matrix, ultimately activating multiple slip systems. Based on the 
above analysis, owing to the formation of a multi-heterogeneous structure induced by YWO@W precursor, the YWO@W-2000 alloy 
achieves a simultaneous enhancement in both strength and ductility.
The cellular structures formed during AM are primarily caused by repeated thermal cycling and solute segregation ( Ren et al., 
2023 ). Unlike 316 L austenitic stainless steel, 9Cr ODS martensitic steel undergoes a complete solid-state phase transformation 
sequence of solidification →δ -ferrite → austenite → martensite ( Yang et al., 2026 ). The cellular structures formed during the solidifi -
cation stage are inherited and reconstructed during the subsequent martensitic shear transformation, eventually evolving into 
dislocation walls. Therefore, the strengthening and toughening contribution provided by the cellular structures in martensite is 
relatively limited ( Su et al., 2025 ). The cellular structures mainly contribute to strengthening through dislocation and solid solution 
strengthening. As shown in Fig. 14 , the maximum contribution can reach 345 MPa (dislocation and solid solution strengthening). 
However, considering that martensitic transformation also introduces significant dislocations, the strengthening contribution from 
cellular structures is far < 345 MPa. In contrast, YWO NPs and their induced heterogeneous structures provide strengthening through 
Orowan and back stress mechanisms, achieving 480 MPa.

significantly pin the matrix grain boundaries, thereby introducing a high density of high-angle grain boundaries. Relevant studies ( Yin 
et al., 2023 ) have shown that abundant high-angle grain boundaries can contribute to higher high-temperature tensile strength.

4. Conclusion
In this work, we present a novel additive manufacturing strategy to overcome the strength-ductility trade-off in 9Cr ODS alloys, and 
the strengthening and toughening mechanisms are thoroughly investigated, leading to the following conclusions: 
(1) During AM, the W shell effectively counteracts the density mismatch between Y
6
WO
12 
and the matrix, preventing the floating of 
YWO NPs. The oxide nanoparticles, uniformly distributed in the YWO@W alloy matrix, have an average size of ~25 nm, which 
contributes significantly to the strength via the Orowan mechanism.
(2) The YWO@W precursor induces the formation of a multi-heterogeneous structure, including coarse/fine grains (CG/FG), high/ 
low defect zones (HDZ/LDZ), non-deformation /deformation zones (NDZ/DZ) and YWO@W/YWO NPs. Asynchronous 
martensitic transformation results in the formation of heterogeneous defect zones. At the same time, uniformly distributed 
YWO@W/YWO NPs induce the formation of FG by acting as heterogeneous nucleation sites. The grain boundary pinning effect 
of YWO/YWO@W NPs results in the formation of serrated grain boundaries and NDZ in the low Schmid factor grain.
(3) The high-density grain boundaries in the FG zones, dislocation tangles in the HDZ, the low Schmid factor and serrated grain 
boundaries in the NDZ, along with the YWO/YWO@W NPs, play a critical role in hindering dislocation slip. Meanwhile, the 
back stress generated at the interfaces of the heterogeneous structures further impedes the dislocation motion, significantly 
enhancing the strength. Moreover, the LDZ and DZ facilitate dislocation storage and slip, while the YWO/YWO@W NPs pro -
mote dislocation cross-slip. At the same time, the YWO NPs, through non-coherent interface with the matrix, may induce 
dislocation slip along the {1 2 1} < 1 1 1 > slip system, ultimately significantly improving the ductility.
(4) Compared with the Y
2
O
3 
sample, the contributions to yield strength from the multi-heterogeneous matrix (back stress) and the 
YWO@W/YWO NPs (Orowan dispersion strengthening) of YWO@W-2000 sample are 357 MPa and 123 MPa, respectively. As a 
result, the YWO@W-2000 alloy exhibits outstanding mechanical properties: an ultimate tensile strength of 1214 MPa and an 
elongation of 10.3%, representing increases of 45.6% and 145.2%, respectively, compared to the Y
2
O
3 
sample.
